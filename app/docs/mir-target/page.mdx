# miR-Target Prediction

miR-Target predicts which genes a miRNA targets. isoTar runs the prediction tools you choose against
the 3′ UTRs of the species, lists every predicted target with the tools that predict it, and shows
where the tools agree. For human, mouse and rat it can then test the predicted genes for pathway and
gene-set enrichment.

/>

## 1. Species

Choose the species; for human, also choose **hg19** or **hg38**.

## 2. miRNA

- **From Catalog:** type part of the name (e.g. `let-7a`) and pick the miRNA. Where it comes from
  more than one precursor, choose the precursor record. The **miRNA Summary** shows the mature
  sequence and where it sits on the precursor.
- **Custom Sequence:** paste your own mature sequence (17–30 nt) and give it a name. A custom
  sequence has no precursor, so the Operation step is skipped.

## 3. Operation (optional)

Change the miRNA to study an isomiR or an edited form. Both sub-steps are optional:

- **Shift** moves the ends of the mature sequence along its precursor. A negative value extends it
  towards the 5′ (left) side, a positive value towards the 3′ (right) side.
- **Modification** changes single bases: give the position on the mature sequence (1-based, counted
  after any shift) and the new base.

The wild type is always predicted too. The variant is predicted as a separate record, and the
results can compare the two (see [Variants](#variants-wild-type-versus-variant)).

## 4. Prediction tools

Choose one or more of **miRanda, miRmap, TargetScan, RNAhybrid, PITA** and **DMISO**. Targets predicted
by several independent tools are usually more reliable, so choose several.

- **TargetScan** is available only for human, mouse, fruit fly, zebrafish and *C. elegans*.
- **Run time** depends mostly on the tools. Genome-wide, miRanda, TargetScan and DMISO take seconds to
  a few minutes, miRmap and PITA several minutes, and **RNAhybrid** can take close to an hour.

## 5. Configuration

The defaults suit almost every job. Two optional cards:

- **Advanced settings:** CPU cores and other run settings.
- **Select Target:** restrict the predictions to genes you name, as gene symbols (`TP53`) or RefSeq
  IDs (`NM_000546`), one per line or comma-separated. isoTar checks each name against the species'
  reference and marks any it cannot find ("not found"); fix or remove those to continue.

<Callout tone="note" title="Leave Select Target empty for enrichment">
  Enrichment tests the job's predicted genes against whole gene-set databases. It needs a
  genome-wide prediction, not a handful of genes.
</Callout>

## 6. Review & Run

The Review step shows exactly what will be submitted. Click **Start Job**; the job page then shows
each tool's progress.

## Reading the results

<TutorialVideo
  name="target-results"
  title="Tutorial 2 of 3: reading the predicted targets, and starting an enrichment analysis."
/>

The **Predicted Targets** tab has three parts:

- **Consensus:** how many targets are predicted by at least 1, 2, 3 … of the tools. The number
  shrinks as you ask for more agreement.
- **Tool overlap (UpSet):** each column is one combination of tools, and its bar counts the targets
  predicted by exactly that combination. The largest 20 combinations are shown.
- **The target table:** one row per target transcript, with its gene symbol, the tools that predict
  it, the gene name and RefSeq ID. It is sorted by the number of tools; click a column header to
  re-sort, and use the filter box to search by gene symbol, gene name or tool.

**Download Results (.zip)** saves every prediction for offline analysis.

### Variants: wild type versus variant

A job with a shift or modification shows a record selector above the table. Pick a record to see its
targets alone, or choose **vs WT** to compare a variant with the wild type. Every target is then
classed as:

| Class | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Shared | Predicted for both, by the same tools |
| Changed | Predicted for both, by a different set of tools |
| Gained | Predicted for the variant only |
| Lost | Predicted for the wild type only |

"Lost" means the variant is **no longer predicted** to bind that target. It is not evidence that the
variant does not bind it.

## Enrichment analysis

<TutorialVideo
  name="target-enrichment"
  title="Tutorial 3 of 3: reading the enrichment results."
/>

Enrichment asks which pathways and gene sets are over-represented among the predicted target genes.
It is available for **miR-Target jobs on human, mouse and rat**. isoTar submits the predicted genes
to [Enrichr](https://maayanlab.cloud/Enrichr/).

1. Open the **Enrichment** tab of a finished job and click **Run Enrichment Analysis**.
2. The **organism** is set by the job's species. Choose the **adjusted P-value cutoff** (default
   0.05) and click **Run**.
3. Enrichment runs in the background and usually takes about a minute. You can leave the page and
   come back.

For a job with a variant, enrichment uses the wild type's predicted genes.

**Reading the results.** A summary line records how many genes were submitted, the organism and the
cutoff. Each tab is one database: **GO Biological Process, GO Cellular Component, GO Molecular
Function, KEGG** and **Reactome**. In each table:

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Term | The pathway or gene set |
| Overlap | Predicted genes in the term / all genes in the term |
| Adjusted P-value | Significance after correction for multiple testing; the table starts with the smallest |
| Combined score | Enrichr's score: log P-value × z-score of the deviation from the expected rank; higher is stronger |
| Genes | The predicted target genes in the term |

The **dotplot** shows the top GO Biological Process terms. A dot further right is more significant
(−log10 of the adjusted P-value), a bigger dot has more genes, and its colour gives the combined
score.

Click **Re-run Enrichment** to try another cutoff. The latest results are saved with the job and
included in its download.

If enrichment fails, the tab shows the error. Check the cutoff and run it again; if it keeps
failing, send us the job ID.
