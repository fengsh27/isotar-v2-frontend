# Getting started

isoTar predicts the targets of a microRNA by running several prediction tools and combining their
results, so you can see which targets the tools agree on. It also scores candidate ceRNA axes
(gene ↔ miRNA ↔ lncRNA) and checks them against the published literature.

## Pick a workflow

Each job analyses **one miRNA**. Choose the workflow by the question you are asking:

| Your question | Workflow | You provide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which genes does my miRNA target? | [miR-Target Prediction](/run?workflow=mir-target) | A miRNA; optionally a list of genes to check |
| Which lncRNAs does my miRNA target? | [miR-LncRNA Prediction](/run?workflow=mir-lncrna) | A miRNA; optionally a list of lncRNAs to check |
| Is this gene–lncRNA pair a ceRNA axis through my miRNA? | [miR-Network Visualization](/network) | A miRNA and up to 100 gene–lncRNA pairs |

The first two answer "what does it target?". The network workflow tests hypotheses you already have,
for example gene–lncRNA pairs whose expression is correlated in your data. Walkthroughs with
tutorial videos: [miR-Target & enrichment](/docs/mir-target) and
[miR-Network Visualization](/docs/mir-network).

## Your first run in five steps

1. **Choose the species.** For human, also choose the genome reference, **hg19** or **hg38**. We
   recommend hg38, especially for lncRNAs: its lncRNA reference holds about 36,000 lncRNA genes,
   against about 14,000 for hg19.
2. **Choose the miRNA** from the catalog for that species (type part of the name, e.g. `miR-21`).
   In miR-Target and miR-LncRNA you can instead enter your own mature sequence (17–30 nt) with a name.
3. **Optionally change the miRNA.** A *shift* moves the mature sequence along its precursor; a
   *modification* changes single positions (e.g. an editing site). isoTar then predicts the
   wild type and the variant side by side.
4. **Choose prediction tools** (miR-Target and miR-LncRNA). The network workflow always runs every
   tool available for the species, so its scores are comparable between jobs.
5. **Review & Run.** The Review step shows exactly what will be submitted. A submitted job cannot be
   changed; to try different settings, start a new job.

## Supported species and tools

isoTar covers 10 species: human, mouse, rat, rhesus macaque, chimpanzee, dog, gray short-tailed
opossum, zebrafish, fruit fly and *C. elegans*.

It runs six prediction tools: **miRanda, miRmap, TargetScan, RNAhybrid, PITA** and **DMISO**.
TargetScan has two limits, because it scores against its own precomputed 3′ UTR alignments instead
of isoTar's reference sequences:

- It is available only for **human, mouse, fruit fly, zebrafish** and ***C. elegans***.
- It never runs on **lncRNAs**: miR-LncRNA jobs and the lncRNA side of network jobs use the other
  five tools.

## Jobs and results

A job runs in the background; the job page shows each tool's progress and opens the results when it
finishes. You can close the page and come back later.

<Callout tone="warning" title="Keep your job link">
  The **Jobs** page lists the jobs submitted from *this browser* only. To open a result on another
  computer, or to share it with a colleague, keep the job page's link (it ends in the job ID).
</Callout>

Results depend on the workflow:

- **miR-Target / miR-LncRNA:** a table of predicted targets (gene label and Ensembl gene and
  transcript IDs) with the tools that predict each one, and a Venn or UpSet diagram of tool
  agreement. Many lncRNAs have no gene label; they are listed by their Ensembl IDs. A job with a variant can be compared with its
  wild type.
- **Enrichment** (miR-Target only, for human, mouse and rat): pathway and gene-set enrichment of
  the predicted target genes; see [Enrichment analysis](/docs/mir-target#enrichment-analysis).
- **miR-Network:** a graph of the gene ↔ miRNA ↔ lncRNA pairs the miRNA bridges, ranked by ceRNA
  score, with optional literature verification.

Every job can be downloaded as a zip of its results. Something not working as expected? Check the
[FAQ](/docs/faq).
